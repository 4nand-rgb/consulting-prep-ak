# 2. Core Consulting Frameworks

## MECE

MECE means Mutually Exclusive and Collectively Exhaustive.

It helps break a problem into clear parts without overlap or missing areas.

Example:
Why are NHS waiting lists increasing?

Possible MECE structure:
1. Demand factors
2. Capacity factors
3. Process factors
4. Technology factors

---

## Pyramid Principle

The Pyramid Principle means starting with the main answer first, then supporting it with key arguments and evidence.

Structure:
1. Main recommendation
2. Key reasons
3. Supporting evidence

Example:
Recommendation: Reduce NHS waiting times through operational redesign and diagnostic expansion.

Supporting points:
1. Demand exceeds capacity
2. Diagnostic delays create bottlenecks
3. Better scheduling can improve productivity

---

## SCQA Framework

SCQA stands for:
- Situation
- Complication
- Question
- Answer

Example:
Situation: The NHS faces rising demand.
Complication: Waiting lists remain high despite funding and reform efforts.
Question: How can the NHS reduce waiting times efficiently?
Answer: Improve patient flow, diagnostic capacity, and workforce productivity.

---

## Porter Five Forces

Porter Five Forces analyses industry competition and attractiveness.

Five forces:
1. Competitive rivalry
2. Threat of new entrants
3. Supplier power
4. Buyer power
5. Threat of substitutes

Healthcare example:
For digital health suppliers entering the NHS, buyer power is high because the NHS is a major purchaser, regulation is strict, and integration requirements are complex.

---

## SWOT Analysis

SWOT stands for:
- Strengths
- Weaknesses
- Opportunities
- Threats

NHS waiting list example:

Strengths:
- National healthcare infrastructure
- Skilled workforce
- Public trust

Weaknesses:
- Capacity pressure
- Staff shortages
- Operational bottlenecks

Opportunities:
- Digital health
- Diagnostic expansion
- Private sector partnerships

Threats:
- Rising demand
- Funding pressure
- Staff burnout

---

## Issue Trees

Issue trees break a large problem into smaller logical parts.

Example question:
Why are NHS waiting times increasing?

Issue tree:
1. Capacity constraints
   - workforce shortage
   - bed shortage
   - diagnostic shortage
2. Rising demand
   - ageing population
   - chronic disease
   - referral growth
3. Operational inefficiency
   - scheduling problems
   - patient flow delays
   - missed appointments
4. Technology limitations
   - fragmented systems
   - poor data visibility

---

## Hypothesis-Driven Thinking

Consultants start with a possible answer and test it with evidence.

Example:
Problem: NHS waiting lists are increasing.
Hypothesis: Demand is growing faster than operational capacity.
Evidence needed:
- referral volumes
- waiting time data
- workforce data
- diagnostic capacity
- bed occupancy

---

## Market Entry Framework

Used to decide whether a company should enter a new market.

Key questions:
1. Is the market attractive?
2. Can the company compete?
3. Is entry financially viable?
4. What risks exist?
5. What entry route is best?

Healthcare example:
A digital health company entering the NHS must assess demand, procurement barriers, interoperability, regulation, and clinical value.

---

## Growth Strategy Framework

Growth options:
1. Acquire new customers
2. Increase usage
3. Launch new services
4. Enter new markets
5. Improve pricing
6. Build partnerships

Community pharmacy example:
A pharmacy can grow through vaccination services, travel clinics, chronic disease support, and online ordering.

---

## Operational Improvement Framework

Operational improvement focuses on making processes faster, safer, cheaper, and more effective.

Core areas:
1. Process efficiency
2. Capacity utilisation
3. Workforce productivity
4. Technology enablement
5. Cost reduction

NHS example:
Improve referral management, theatre scheduling, diagnostics, discharge planning, and patient flow.

---

## Stakeholder Analysis

Stakeholder analysis identifies who is affected by a project and how they should be managed.

NHS digital transformation stakeholders:
- Patients
- Doctors
- Nurses
- NHS managers
- IT teams
- Regulators
- Suppliers
- Government bodies

Key questions:
1. Who is affected?
2. What do they care about?
3. How much influence do they have?
4. Are they supportive or resistant?
5. How should they be engaged?

# 3. Case Interview Preparation

## Profitability Cases

Core formula:

Profit = Revenue - Cost

Revenue drivers:
- Price
- Volume
- Product mix

Cost drivers:
- Fixed costs
- Variable costs

Healthcare example:

## Market Sizing Cases

Basic structure:

Market Size = Number of Customers × Average Spending

Example:
Estimate the UK community pharmacy flu vaccine market.

Inputs:
- UK population
- eligible adults
- vaccination rate
- average revenue per vaccine

# 5. Portfolio Project 1: NHS Waiting List Opportunity

## Objective

Analyse NHS waiting list pressures and identify operational improvement opportunities using consulting frameworks, healthcare strategy, and analytics.

## Key Questions

1. What factors are increasing waiting lists?
2. Which specialties face the highest pressure?
3. Where are operational bottlenecks?
4. Which interventions could improve capacity?
5. What role can digital health and private partnerships play?

## Problem Statement

NHS waiting lists have increased due to rising demand, operational pressure, workforce shortages, diagnostic bottlenecks, and limited capacity.

The core problem is that demand for healthcare services is increasing faster than operational capacity.

This creates:
- treatment delays
- patient dissatisfaction
- workforce pressure
- operational inefficiencies
- financial pressure

## Key Drivers

1. Workforce shortages
2. Diagnostic delays
3. Bed capacity limitations
4. Referral growth
5. Operational inefficiencies
6. Technology limitations

## Initial Hypothesis

Operational inefficiencies and capacity constraints are major contributors to increasing NHS waiting times.

## Analysis Structure

### Demand Analysis

Assess:
- referral growth
- ageing population
- chronic disease burden
- patient activity trends

### Capacity Analysis

Assess:
- workforce availability
- bed capacity
- diagnostic capacity
- theatre utilisation

### Process Analysis

Assess:
- patient flow
- scheduling efficiency
- referral management
- discharge delays

### Technology Analysis

Assess:
- data visibility
- EHR integration
- digital scheduling
- automation opportunities

## Potential Solutions

1. Improve scheduling and capacity planning
2. Expand diagnostic capacity
3. Use digital dashboards for visibility
4. Redesign patient pathways
5. Reduce missed appointments
6. Improve discharge processes
7. Use private sector partnerships selectively
8. Forecast demand using analytics

## Expected Consulting Output

A consultant-style report including:
- problem definition
- root cause analysis
- data-backed findings
- strategic recommendations
- implementation roadmap
- expected impact
